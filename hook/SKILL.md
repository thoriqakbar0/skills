# Hook

Give the intended reader a reason to look, a new reason to keep reading, and a payoff worth the time. Take as much editorial freedom as the brief allows. Rebuild the piece around its strongest material when line edits would leave the structure weak.

## Find the lead

Read the whole draft and any supplied source material. Identify what this reader already knows, what they care about, and which true detail would most change what they expect. Look for buried consequences, decisions with something at stake, specific failures, telling scenes, or evidence that overturns an assumption. Match the hook to the reader's reason for being there. Someone looking for an answer may keep reading precisely because you give it to them immediately.

Draft materially different leads in private when there is a real choice. Try changing what the reader learns first, not just how the same fact is phrased. Compare each lead with the next few lines in the actual format, including the headline or preview when supplied. Pick the one that earns the next line and sets up a promise the piece can keep. Cut throat-clearing. Give the reader enough context to understand why the lead matters.

## Keep earning the next line

Read in order as someone seeing the piece for the first time. When attention drops, find the cause. If the reader is lost, add the missing context at the point of need. If they can already predict the paragraph, cut it or bring forward the evidence that changes their understanding. If they are waiting too long for an answer, pay it off sooner. Let each paragraph do something new, such as advancing the action, producing evidence, answering a question, or making a consequence concrete.

Reshape before polishing. Move proof to the claim it supports, compress setup, cut repeated points, and build transitions from what the reader just learned to what they now want to know. Vary sentence and paragraph length where it helps the reader feel the pace. Keep breathing room where a detail needs time to land. Protect the author's voice and preserve facts, attribution, and material qualifiers. Earn tension from what is actually there.

## Land it

Return to the opening's promise. End on the answer, consequence, decisive image, or next action that fulfills it. Once it lands, stop. If the strongest available ending cannot satisfy the opening, change the opening.

Read the revised piece at normal speed. Check where a reader could leave, what they would take away if they did, and whether the strongest line still holds up against the source. Revise the weakest stretch while a change can earn more attention without distorting the piece. Return the rewritten prose by default. Show alternatives or editorial notes when requested.
