# Copywrite

## Identify the reader's decision

Use the brief and available evidence to identify the reader, what brought them here, what they already know, and the next action they can reasonably take. Use customer language from interviews, reviews, or support logs when available. Treat inferred motivations and objections as hypotheses. If the brief is thin, proceed with a stated assumption and keep unsupported claims out of the copy.

Write the value proposition for that decision in one sentence. Answer why this reader should take this action with this offer over their most relevant alternative, including doing nothing. Look for an outcome they want, a meaningful difference, and a fact that makes it believable. Check the proposition for appeal, exclusivity, credibility, and clarity. If the difference is unknown, describe the specific value you can substantiate and flag the missing evidence.

## Diagnose before rewriting

Use the MECLABS Conversion Sequence Heuristic, `C = 4m + 3v + 2(i − f) − 2a`, as a qualitative diagnostic. Its coefficients express relative emphasis. Avoid numeric scores or lift forecasts based on the formula.

- **Motivation (m).** Match the promise and ask to the reader's reason for arriving. Carry the promise from the ad or email through to the destination.
- **Value proposition (v).** State the strongest relevant benefit early. Make the difference concrete and put proof near the claim it supports.
- **Incentive (i).** Use a genuine incentive when it offsets a specific cost of acting.
- **Friction (f).** Remove unnecessary work, choices, or steps where the task permits. Where work is necessary, make the commitment clear upfront.
- **Anxiety (a).** Answer the specific concern where it arises. A verified billing term near payment is more useful than generic reassurance.

If the bottleneck is the offer or process, identify the specific change it needs and deliver the strongest copy the current facts support.

## Draft through the decision

Order the message by what the reader needs to decide. Establish relevance, explain the benefit and difference, support the claim, and make the next action clear. Treat each step as a small agreement that earns the next one. For example, an email can earn a click with a relevant promise and leave the page to explain the full offer. A signup button should name what happens next and match the actual destination.

Use verified terms, numbers, testimonials, and guarantees. Disclose material conditions where they affect the choice. Keep proof gaps in editorial notes outside publish-ready copy.

## Deliver the requested artifact

- For drafting or rewriting, lead with the finished copy in channel order. Mention assumptions or missing proof only when they affect use.
- For an audit, lead with the highest-impact issue you can support. Quote the affected copy, explain the reader's likely hesitation, and show the replacement.
- For a test, state what changes, which reader hesitation it addresses, and the conversion event to measure. Make variants test distinct hypotheses. Treat any expected improvement as a hypothesis until measured.
